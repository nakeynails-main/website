Your nails are splitting because of how acrylics are filed on

It's not the acrylic itself. It's what happens before it goes on.

You go to your appointment excited for a fresh set. Two hours later, your nails feel thicker, stronger, invincible. Then four weeks pass. You look down and see splits running vertically down your nail beds, especially near the sides. Brittle edges that catch on everything. Nails that feel paper-thin underneath where the acrylic used to be. This is not normal wear. This is damage from the application process itself.

When an acrylic nail is applied, the technician has to file your natural nail down significantly to create a surface for the product to stick. That filing is aggressive. A nail file moving in one direction across your nail plate is abrasive by design, and when done repeatedly to create the right texture for acrylic adhesion, it strips away protective layers of your nail. But here is the part nobody talks about: the pressure and direction of that filing matters enormously. Horizontal filing across the nail edge causes the keratin layers to separate and splinter. This is literally creating the conditions for nail splitting and breakage before the acrylic even goes on.

The damage accelerates because acrylic application involves more than just filing. Technicians push, manipulate, and reshape your nail during the sculpting process. Your nail plate is being held at angles it does not naturally sit in. The acrylic hardens while your nail is under this pressure, and when you finally remove it weeks later, your nail has essentially been reshaped and weakened in the process. The nail splitting you see after removal is the result of structural damage that happened during application, not after.

Why acrylic filing causes splitting specifically

Your nail is made of tightly packed keratin layers that overlap like shingles on a roof. When a file moves across these layers incorrectly, it separates them instead of smoothing them. Nail splitting and breakage happens when those layers no longer bond properly. This is why you can look at your damaged nail and literally see horizontal lines or peeling. The file has broken the seal between layers.

The problem is compounded if your technician files in multiple directions or applies too much pressure. Some technicians will file the edge, then file the top, then file the sides, each time further compromising the structure. Your nail cannot sustain this level of manipulation without consequences. Add in the chemical exposure from the acrylic primer and adhesive, and the nail plate becomes increasingly brittle and prone to splitting.

How your nails split after the acrylic comes off

The moment the acrylic is removed, your nail is already damaged. But the real splitting often happens in the weeks that follow because your nail has lost its protective outer layer and its internal strength. It is now thinner, more porous, and much more likely to break horizontally or split vertically. You might notice splits forming right at the edge, or sometimes deeper into the nail near the nail bed. This is your nail showing you it cannot support itself yet.

Nail splitting and breakage can also continue if you are too rough during your nail care routine. After acrylic removal, your nails need gentleness. Clipping too close to the quick, using metal files instead of gentler alternatives, or exposing them to harsh chemicals will worsen splits that are already forming.

Rebuilding nails after splitting

Recovery takes time. You cannot rush nails that have been compromised by aggressive filing during acrylic application. What you can do is protect them while they grow out. Keep them short so the damaged sections do not catch and split further. Moisturize consistently, especially around the nail bed and cuticle area. Avoid water exposure when possible, and wear gloves during cleaning or dishwashing.

Support your nails from the inside out with a nail repair serum like NakeyPen, which is designed to restore keratin and strengthen nails that have been damaged by acrylic and gel application. Apply it daily to your nail beds and cuticles, letting the peptides and hydrating ingredients rebuild the structure that filing damaged. In about eight to twelve weeks, you should see new healthy growth at the base of your nail. The damaged section will eventually grow out completely.

The truth about nail splitting and breakage after acrylics is that it does not have to happen. The key is finding a technician who files conservatively, files in one direction only, and does not apply unnecessary pressure. Your nails are not weak. They have just been treated too roughly.
