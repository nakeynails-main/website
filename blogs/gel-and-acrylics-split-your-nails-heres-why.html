Gel and acrylics split your nails. Here's why it happens and how to stop it.

The pressure and chemicals behind the breaks are avoidable.

You finally get your gel or acrylic nails removed and notice something unsettling. Your nail edges are jagged. There are splits running down the sides. Some nails have broken clean in half. This isn't random breakage. Gel and acrylic nails cause splitting because of how they're applied, how they're removed, and what happens to your nail structure underneath.

The splitting starts the moment your nail technician files your natural nail down to apply the base layer. That aggressive filing weakens the edge of your nail and removes the protective outer layer. Your nail plate becomes thinner and more vulnerable to pressure before anything is even added on top.

The pressure that creates splits

When gel or acrylic is applied, there's constant downward pressure as your technician shapes and files the enhancement. They're not just sculpting the product. They're also pressing it against your natural nail, which flexes your nail bed and stresses the edges. If your natural nail is already brittle or dehydrated, it splits under this pressure instead of bending with it.

Acrylics create even more stress because they're heavier and stiffer than gel. That weight pulls on your nail edges with every hand movement. The flexing is minimal, so the stress concentrates at weak points. Over time, these pressure points become actual splits that run from your cuticle down toward your fingertip.

Gel and acrylic nails cause splitting in another way too. Both products seal moisture out of your nail. Your nail becomes dehydrated and brittle underneath the enhancement. When you remove them, that brittle nail is suddenly exposed to air and pressure again. It splits because it has no flexibility left.

Why removal makes it worse

The moment you remove gel or acrylic, the splitting often gets worse. If your technician files off the enhancement, they're filing right into those weakened areas again. If you soak in acetone, the solvent strips away even more of your nail's protective barrier. Your nail is now thin, dehydrated, and exposed.

You peel or pick at loosened gel and acrylics without thinking about it. That peeling motion forces the split deeper into your nail. What started as a small edge split becomes a crack that runs down the center of the nail. Some people don't see the full damage until weeks later when new growth reveals how badly the structure was compromised.

Splitting happens before you realize it

The tricky part about splits from gel and acrylic nails is that you often don't see them until the enhancement is already off. Your nail might be splitting underneath but looking perfectly smooth on top. By the time you notice, the damage goes deeper than you thought.

Frequent visits to the salon speed this up. If you're getting gel or acrylic refills every two to three weeks, your nails never get a break. They're constantly being filed, pressured, rehydrated less than they should be, and split further. After six months of regular appointments, your nails might be so compromised that they break at the slightest bump.

Rebuilding nails after splits

Recovery starts by leaving your nails alone. No gel. No acrylic. No polish if you can manage it. Your nail needs to grow out without any product sitting on top adding pressure or moisture loss. This takes months. A full nail grows out in four to six months depending on your growth rate.

While you're growing them out, hydration matters more than anything else. Your nails split because they're dry and brittle. A targeted repair serum with peptides and hydrating ingredients helps your nail rebuild its structure from the inside. Something like NakeyPen works during this growth phase to strengthen new nail as it emerges and help existing damage heal faster.

Avoid filing your nails into thin shapes. Keep them short and slightly rounded to reduce stress at the edges. Wear gloves when washing dishes or cleaning. Water exposure without protection makes splitting worse. The more you can protect your nails while they're fragile, the faster they can rebuild without fresh splits forming.
