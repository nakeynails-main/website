Gel and acrylics are breaking your nails. Here's why.

It is not just removal. The application itself causes real damage.

You sit down in the chair for what should be a relaxing manicure. Two hours later, you have fresh gel or acrylics. But within days, you notice your natural nails underneath feel weird. Thinner. Weaker. Maybe even splitting at the edges. You are not imagining this. Nail splitting from gel and acrylics happens during application, not just during removal, and most people never realize it.

The damage starts the moment your nail technician begins preparing your nail bed. Buffing is aggressive. Filing the surface to create adhesion removes the protective outer layer of your nail, exposing the more fragile layers beneath. This is not gentle exfoliation. This is deliberate abrasion designed to help products stick. Your nail is already compromised before the gel or acrylic even touches it.

The pressure and heat that weakens nails

Gel and acrylics add significant weight and thickness to your nail. That extra bulk puts constant pressure on the nail bed, which can cause the nail plate itself to split along stress lines. If your technician is not careful during application, pressing and manipulating the product onto your nail can actually bend or flex your nail in ways it was not designed to move. Each flex creates microscopic fractures in the keratin structure.

Then there is the curing process. UV light from gel lamps generates heat, and that heat dries out your nail from the inside. Dehydrated nails are brittle nails, and brittle nails split easily. The longer your nails stay under a gel lamp, the more moisture is stripped away. Some technicians keep nails under the light for extended periods, which only makes the problem worse.

Acrylic nails add another layer of trauma. The chemicals used in acrylic application, particularly the monomer liquid, can penetrate the nail plate and weaken its structure from within. Combined with the aggressive filing during shaping and filing again at the edge, your nail is being sanded down from multiple directions. Nail splitting from gel and acrylics is a direct result of this cumulative friction.

Why your nails keep splitting even after you stop

You finally decide to take a break and remove your gel or acrylics. But your nails continue to split weeks later. This happens because the damage does not stop when the product comes off. Your nail is now thinner and weaker than it was before. It is trying to grow out and heal, but it has to push through nails that are structurally compromised.

The nail splitting continues because your nail bed needs time to regenerate. That outer protective layer that was buffed away needs to regrow. The dehydrated keratin needs to reabsorb moisture. The thin, weakened sections need to grow out completely so they can be trimmed away. This process takes months, not weeks. Many people assume their nails are permanently damaged and go right back to gel or acrylics, which starts the cycle over again.

Rebuilding nails after splitting damage

The good news is that nail splitting damage is reversible, but it requires patience and proper care. Stop buffing your nails aggressively if you decide to get manicures again. Ask your technician to use a gentler file and to avoid over-preparing your nail surface. If you do get gel or acrylics, request shorter application times and lower heat settings on the UV lamp.

At home, your nails need intensive hydration and strengthening support. This is where targeted nail repair serums become essential. Products designed with peptides and keratin can help restore the protein structure of your nail and seal in moisture. Applied daily, they give your nail the nutrients it needs to rebuild thick, strong growth. Combined with keeping your nails moisturized and avoiding harsh chemicals, you can reverse the splitting and get back to healthy nails.

Nail splitting from gel and acrylics is preventable, but only if you understand when the damage actually happens. It is during application, every single time. If you are going to wear these products, go to a skilled technician who takes time with application and uses lower-heat methods. If your nails are already damaged, give them real recovery time and support them with a repair serum like NakeyPen while they heal. Your nails will thank you.
