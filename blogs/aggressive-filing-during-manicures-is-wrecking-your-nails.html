Aggressive filing during manicures is ruining your nails

Your technician's buffing habits are the real culprit behind your damaged nails.

You sit down in the salon chair excited for a fresh gel manicure. The technician starts filing your nails, and you can feel the pressure. It's rough. It's aggressive. You mention it might be a bit much, and they say it's necessary to make the polish stick. Two weeks later, your nails feel thinner than before. This isn't a coincidence. Aggressive filing during gel and acrylic application is one of the most underestimated forms of filing damage that happens in salons every single day.

The problem is that many technicians file way harder than they need to. They're trying to create texture so the gel or acrylic adheres properly, but they end up removing too much of your actual nail plate. This filing damage happens in minutes, but the consequences last for months. Your nails don't just feel weaker. They actually are weaker.

Why technicians file so aggressively

Here's the thing: proper adhesion requires a textured surface. Gel and acrylic won't stick to a smooth, shiny nail plate. So technicians use a file to roughen it up. The issue is that there's a massive difference between light scoring and aggressive buffing. Light scoring means a few gentle strokes. Aggressive buffing means going back and forth repeatedly, creating visible wear on your nail.

Some technicians also file aggressively because they're rushing. The faster they file, the quicker they move through clients. Others do it out of habit or because they were trained that way. Nobody ever taught them that filing damage accumulates. If you get manicures every two to three weeks, you're getting your nails filed aggressively 17 to 26 times per year. That's a lot of wear on your nail plate.

The damage filing causes to your nail structure

Your nail plate has multiple layers, kind of like plywood. When a technician files too hard during application, they're shaving away the outer layers of keratin and protein that protect the nail underneath. This filing damage weakens the structural integrity of your entire nail. Over time, this leads to nails that crack more easily, break at odd angles, and feel paper thin.

The damage is especially severe if you're getting regular gel or acrylic manicures. Each appointment compounds the problem. Your nail plate gets thinner and thinner because there's less healthy nail to file next time. After a few months of aggressive filing during application, your natural nails barely exist underneath the enhancement. Some people have nails so damaged they can't even grow out properly once they stop getting manicures.

What makes this worse is that filing damage isn't always obvious immediately. You might feel fine after the appointment. But two weeks later, your nail beds are sensitive. A month later, your nails peel. Three months later, you're dealing with severe brittleness that takes half a year to recover from.

How to protect your nails from salon filing

The best protection is speaking up in the moment. When your technician starts filing, if it feels rough or aggressive, say something. A good technician will adjust. You can ask them to use a gentler touch or a finer grit file. You can also request that they file less overall. If they're going to do gel or acrylic, they don't need your nails filed down to nothing. A light score is genuinely all that's needed for adhesion.

If you switch salons, pay attention to how different technicians file. Some are noticeably gentler. Those are the ones worth returning to. Before your next appointment, you can also ask your technician what grit file they use. A 240 to 320 grit file is gentler than an 80 to 120 grit, which is often what salons default to.

Between appointments, filing damage means your nails need extra care. Avoid filing your own nails horizontally or vigorously. Keep them moisturized. And if your nails are already thin or peeling from previous appointments, give them a break before your next manicure. Let them recover for at least a few weeks.

Reversing filing damage takes time

If you're already experiencing damage from aggressive filing during application at the salon, the recovery process is slow. You're essentially waiting for your damaged nails to grow out completely and be replaced by healthier nail. Depending on how severe the damage is, this can take four to six months or longer. During that time, your nails will be fragile and prone to peeling or breaking.
